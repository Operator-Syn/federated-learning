Federated Learning for Collaborative Healthcare AI Without Moving Patient Records.

Iligan • barangay health facilities
02The idea

What is federated learning?

Several sites train one shared model without pooling their records. Each site learns from its own data, and only the updates travel back (Pati et al., 2024).

Privacy is architectural, not automatic. Records stay in each facility; only a model update leaves. That update can still reveal information about the data behind it, so encryption, access control, and audit remain necessary.

03Why federate?

Benefits,
with conditions.

01

Learn across sites

A small facility learns from patterns seen elsewhere, including events it has never recorded itself (Wei et al., 2023).

02

Keep records local

Training runs where the records already sit, so fewer copies have to be made.

03

Make collaboration possible

Sites can work together even when moving patient records is difficult or not permitted.

Federation is not free. It adds coordination, network traffic, compute at every site, and a new security duty. Where pooled data is allowed, centralized training is usually simpler.
04Our context

The proposition,
and the setting.

Working proposition

“Federation may let a facility benefit from events it has never experienced itself.”

The study treats this as something to test, not a premise. A low-volume station may have too few examples of a rare event to learn from. If federation helps, the gain should be largest where records are thinnest; if not, the proposition fails.

Candidate tasks

TB referral prioritization · follow-up risk · commodity demand forecasting

Current study

The study compares client-selection policies under intermittent connectivity, using one usable prediction task as the vehicle.

05Scenario · a coastal barangay station

Meet Ma’am
Lorna.

A midwife and officer-in-charge at a coastal barangay station. With one health worker, she keeps paper stock cards and sends monthly requisitions through the RHU.

Today

Demand is seasonal and hard to predict. She over-orders some months and still runs short in others.

With a useful model

A short, explainable forecast fills the requisition she already signs: no new dashboard, no extra routine.

Federation’s role

Patterns learned at other facilities can improve her forecast, especially where her own records hold no example (Wei et al., 2023).

The question this raises. Is ordering the decision the station actually makes, and can a forecast be built from the records it already keeps? Only the health office can confirm both.
06What makes it hard?

Participation
is the constraint.

Facilities differ in bandwidth, power stability, staff time, and data volume. A policy that always picks the fastest clients finishes sooner, but leaves weaker links out (Gao et al., 2022).

Efficiency

How fast does training reach a predefined target, and at what cost in bytes and rounds?

Inclusion

Who takes part, who is repeatedly skipped, and how each facility performs, not just the pooled average.

What the study measures. Time to reach a usable model, and who is left out along the way.

07Research questions

Two questions.

The first asks what to build; the second, what the network does to it.

RQ1

Design

How can a federated learning framework optimize the demand forecasting and inventory management of essential medicines across a City Health Office and resource-constrained Barangay Health Stations, while preserving data privacy and handling intermittent network connectivity?

RQ2

Robustness

How do intermittent internet connections and offline operational periods at remote barangay health stations affect the convergence speed and predictive stability of the city-wide medicine demand model?

08What is required?

What it takes,
in numbers.

Every site requirement becomes a number in the methods. The software layer stays a skeleton, because the platform is still open.

Software — skeleton

  • Data contract — one schema for site and coordinator
  • Client stub — wraps the site’s existing training script
  • Coordinator — schedules rounds, collects updates
  • Aggregation — weighted averaging, first implementation
  • Logging and transport — stubs until a platform is chosen

Hardware (Gao et al., 2022)

  • Disk — GB for the local extract and one training batch
  • Memory — GB held during a training pass
  • Compute — minutes for one local training pass
  • Upload — bytes per model update, measured once per site
  • Link uptime — share of scheduled rounds the site is online
09Choosing the platform

Which platform fits?

Federated learning is a protocol, not a product. Several open frameworks can run these experiments, so the choice should follow our requirements.

What the study needs

  • Custom client selection
  • Dropouts, timeouts, and retries on weak links
  • Per-site metrics and repeatable runs
  • Auditable logs and secure transport

Still in the running

  • NVFlare, Flower, OpenFL — general FL orchestration
  • TensorFlow Federated and Substra — training frameworks
  • FATE and PySyft — privacy-preserving computation
  • CODA — healthcare federation built on FHIR (Mullie et al., 2024)
