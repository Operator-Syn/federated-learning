\section{Selection policy families}
\label{sec:policies}

The study does not propose a new selection algorithm. It instantiates the families that
already exist and measures what each costs under Iligan-like connectivity. Four families are
compared; the fourth is a baseline included on purpose. Uniform random selection is what the
other three have to beat. Without that neutral comparison, a better result could come from an
easier comparison rather than from the policy.

\subsection{Naming rule}
The four names above are normative; the thesis and the review of related literature (RRL) are to
use the same four. Wherever the implementation distinguishes the families — in its configuration,
in its reports, in its records — it uses these names and no others, so that a policy discussed
here can be found unambiguously in the system that runs it. Changing which family is compared
is then a change of configuration rather than a rebuild of the system. A fifth name, or a
family called one thing in a report and another in a configuration file, is a defect in the
implementation.

\subsection{What a policy may not do}
No policy may select a facility and later report it as participating when it did not
answer. Selection and participation are recorded separately
(Section~\ref{sec:entities-system}), so that a wasted slot shows up in the run's record instead
of being quietly refilled.
